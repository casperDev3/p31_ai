We have a saying in this valley: an apple that travels three weeks tastes like the truck it rode in on. We don't make ours travel. From the moment our pickers walk the rows at first light, the clock is running — two hours to cool the fruit, twenty-four to grade and crate, and another twenty-four to put it on your table.

"The shortest distance between a tree and a kitchen is the one most worth taking."

This issue catalogues our harvest: eight varieties, four of them heritage cultivars saved from regional extinction, four of them modern crosses bred for crisp and keep. No waxes, no controlled-atmosphere chambers, no third-party packers diluting the chain. What you receive is what we picked, on the day you placed your order.

— M. Halloran

The Method

§ III · Method
Picked to packed in XXIV h
i
Step One · Field

Hand-picked

Pickers walk the rows daily during peak window. Only fruit at the right sugar comes down — measured by refractometer, twice per row.

ii
Step Two · House

Sorted on-site

Optical sizing followed by a manual second pass. Bruised or undersized fruit becomes cider — never waste, never landfill.

iii
Step Three · Cold

Cold-packed

Pre-cooled to 2°C within two hours of picking. Packed into ventilated wooden crates lined with recycled pulp.

iv
Step Four · Road

Direct-shipped

Refrigerated truck, or bicycle courier within town. No warehouse middlemen. No supermarket shelf time. No detours.
